- Method: open the retailer listing for the exact product and size, expand the Ingredients block, and copy the printed text verbatim (Active and Inactive lists as printed).
- Variant rule: a listing was used only when the name, size, and variant matched. ## Anomalies

- Brand-site partial lists omitted most of each full list. Examples: the Healing Ointment brand text was "Petrolatum (41%)" only. The retailer printed mineral oil, ceresin, lanolin alcohol, panthenol, glycerin, and bisabolol.
- The Lip Repair ointment 2-pack and the single tube print different inactive lists (the 2-pack omits lecithin, ascorbyl palmitate, glyceryl stearate, glyceryl oleate, citric acid). Each row uses its own printed list.
- Dimethicone appears in the Lip Repair Stick (Shea Butter) line, not in its brand-site partial list. The reference marks dimethicone PFAS-treated, so those four lip-stick rows escalate to High Priority Swap.
- Phenoxyethanol and sodium cetearyl sulfate appear in every verified cream and lotion. Phenoxyethanol is flagged cat. 1, 2, 4, 5, 8 in the reference, which places all seven creams and lotions in High Priority Swap under the leave-on rule.
- Lip Protectant + SPF 30 is an SPF product labelled as a lip protectant. Petrolatum (cat. 1) in a leave-on sunscreen escalates to High Priority Swap, matching the rule applied in the first pass.
- The Children's Itch Relief Ointment is an OTC drug-label product with hydrocortisone (1%) as the active. Hydrocortisone is not in the reference and is in the manual review queue.
- Walmart Diaper Rash Paste 3.5 oz printed Zinc Oxide 40.0%. The reference lists zinc oxide (non-nano) as low tox in creams, and nano status is not stated, so it was graded as that entry.
- The 0.25 oz Healing Ointment label reads "Panthenol Glycerin" without a comma. Graded as two ingredients; kept as printed in the verbatim column.
- The Aloe Vera and Coconut lip sticks print a "1% skin safe synthetic" line. It is a labelling note, not an ingredient; it was removed from the grading input only.
- Grading notes: the grader was patched for this pass to handle drug-label prefixes ("Active:", "Inactive:", "Purpose:") and the lip and sunscreen leave-on escalation. The grader file itself (_grader.py) is unchanged.
