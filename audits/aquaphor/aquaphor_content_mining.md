# Aquaphor Audit — Content Mining Report

**Date audited:** 2026-10-16
**Products audited:** 41 (full catalog at https://www.aquaphorus.com/products)
**Source:** https://www.aquaphorus.com/ (Aquaphor US site, Beiersdorf)

**Data caveat:** The brand site displays only a partial ingredient block per product: the first few INCI names, or the active-ingredient list for OTC-format labels. No full INCI list is shown on any product page, and "View all" does not expand it. Every row is `needs review` and every tier is provisional. Re-grade against the packaging before any of these numbers go into a caption.

---

## Section A — Brand-level callout

**Headline finding:** Of 41 Aquaphor products, 18 (44%) contain petrolatum or mineral oil, which the reference lists as category 1, in leave-on products, and so grade Avoid (High Priority Swap) under the leave-on carcinogen rule; the other 19 show no flags on the partial lists the site displays.

**Tier distribution (provisional):**
- Puberty Safe: 19 (46.3%)
- Use Caution: 2 (4.9%)
- Avoid (Mild): 2 (4.9%)
- Avoid (Moderate): 0 (0%)
- Avoid (High Priority Swap): 18 (43.9%)

**Single most-flagged product:** Aquaphor® Lip Protectant + SPF 30 and Aquaphor® Lip Protectant + SPF (Dual Pack) — 6 flags each. Petrolatum (cat. 1) plus five SPF filters (avobenzone, octinoxate, octisalate, octocrylene, oxybenzone; cat. 2). Escalated to High Priority Swap for petrolatum in a leave-on product.

**Cleanest product (if any):** Aquaphor® Moisturizing Cream (8oz.) — Puberty Safe. Displayed list is water, glycerin, caprylic/capric triglyceride, with no flagged ingredients. Three names are not in the reference and are listed as unknown. Full list not on the page.

**Content angle for Section A:**
"I scanned all 41 Aquaphor products. The one ointment everyone owns is a petrolatum product, and the lip SPF line scored highest on flags." Caveat before posting: the brand site shows partial ingredient lists, so pair this with a packaging check.

## Section B — Pattern callouts

**Most common flagged ingredient:** Petrolatum — appears in 18 of 41 products (all healing ointments, baby ointments, balm sticks, both lip-protectant SPF products, and both body sprays).

**Most common risk category:** Category 1 (Carcinogen) — 22 flag entries (18 petrolatum, 2 mineral oil, 2 PEG-200 inferred). Category 2 (Endocrine Disruption) — 20 flag entries, almost all from the SPF filters.

**"Clean marketing, dirty formula" finds:**
- The Lip Protectant + SPF products are sold as everyday lip protection. Their petrolatum and five SPF filters put them in High Priority Swap, the highest tier.
- The Moisturizing Cream's copy says it is "free of fragrance, parabens and dyes" and "hypoallergenic." It grades Puberty Safe on the displayed list, but the full INCI is not shown, so this is a formula to verify, not a clean finding.

**Repeated naming patterns:**
- Every petrolatum product carries the same category-1 flag: 18 of 18 with petrolatum at 30–41%. These are the healing ointment family.
- Every SPF product on the site lists four to six UV filters from the same family (avobenzone, octisalate, octocrylene, homosalate, octinoxate, oxybenzone).
- Every Baby Healing Ointment and Healing Ointment size is the same single active (petrolatum 41%) across sizes.

**Content angles for Section B:**
1. "One ingredient, 18 products": a single-ingredient ointment line that spans baby, kids, and adult sizes. Show that the ingredient is the same across sizes and sales channels.
2. "Sunscreen in the lip balm": the lip products that carry SPF stack five UV filters onto a lip balm, which many parents do not expect.
3. "Partial ingredient lists": the brand site does not publish full INCI lists, so the audit only sees what is displayed. Good for a process post on why ingredient transparency matters.

## Section C — Comparison angles

**Best vs worst within categories:**
- Lip products: Best = Aquaphor® Lip Repair Stick (Puberty Safe, displayed list). Worst = Aquaphor® Lip Protectant + SPF 30 and Dual Pack (Avoid, High Priority Swap; 6 flags).
- Leave-on creams and lotions: Best = any Hydrating Daily Lotion or Moisturizing Cream (all Puberty Safe on displayed list). Worst = none (all Puberty Safe, though unknown ingredients remain).
- Ointments and balm sticks: Best = none (all High Priority Swap, petrolatum). Worst = all of them.
- Body wash/shampoo: Best = Aquaphor® Baby Wash & Shampoo (Use Caution, one inferred PEG flag). Worst = same (the two sizes are identical in tier).
- Sunscreen lip stick: Best = Aquaphor® Lip Repair Stick + Sunscreen (Avoid Mild, 4 filters, no petrolatum). Worst = Lip Protectant + SPF 30 (High Priority Swap).

**Similar marketing, different grades:**
- Moisturizing Cream vs Healing Ointment: both are marketed as skin barrier care for dry, cracked skin. The cream is Puberty Safe on its displayed list. The ointment is High Priority Swap for petrolatum.
- Lip Repair Stick vs Lip Protectant + SPF: same brand, same product format, same "lip repair" framing. One is Puberty Safe; the SPF version is High Priority Swap.

**Cleanest in each category:**
- Creams and lotions: Aquaphor® Moisturizing Cream (8oz.) or any Hydrating Daily Lotion size (Puberty Safe; zero flags on displayed list).
- Lip products without SPF: Aquaphor® Lip Repair Stick Naturally Inspired (Aloe Vera or Coconut) and Aquaphor® Lip Repair (Puberty Safe; zero flags on displayed list).
- Body wash: Aquaphor® Baby Wash & Shampoo (Use Caution; one inferred flag).

**Content angles for Section C:**
1. "The same brand, two lip balms": the Lip Repair Stick and the SPF lip protectant sit side by side on the site and score in different tiers.
2. "Cream or ointment?": the site's own FAQ says to choose the ointment for "very dry, cracked" areas. That is the product family that carries petrolatum.
3. "Lotion vs ointment for the same kid": a parent who uses the Baby Sensitive Lotion and the Baby Healing Ointment on the same child gets two different tiers from one brand.

Water and glycerin are not in the reference PDF and are routed to manual review, not treated as low tox. Hydrocortisone is in Aquaphor Children's Itch Relief Ointment and is not in the reference. It is the one unknown that needs a pharmacology look before the product is described.

**Reformulation flags:** None observed. The site notes that "ingredients ... are updated regularly" and that packaging may differ from the website. That discrepancy is the reason every row is `needs review`.

**Naturally occurring allergens:** None. The displayed lists contain no essential-oil components.
